Dental Implants

Permanent tooth replacement with titanium implants. Restore your natural smile and confidence with cutting-edge implant technology.

What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are titanium posts surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as artificial tooth roots. They provide a strong foundation for fixed or removable replacement teeth that are made to match your natural teeth. Implants are the most advanced and long-lasting solution for missing teeth, offering both functional and aesthetic benefits.

The Procedure

1

Consultation

Comprehensive oral examination, X-rays, and 3D imaging to assess bone density and plan your treatment.

2

Planning

Custom treatment plan created using digital technology for precise implant placement and optimal results.

3

Surgery

Minimally invasive implant placement under local anesthesia. The titanium post is precisely positioned into the jawbone.

4

Healing

Osseointegration period where the implant fuses with the bone. Temporary restoration provided during this phase.

5

Crown Placement

Custom-crafted porcelain crown attached to the implant, perfectly matching your natural teeth in color and shape.

Benefits

  • Natural look and feel — indistinguishable from real teeth
  • Long-lasting solution — implants can last a lifetime with proper care
  • Preserves jawbone and prevents bone loss
  • No impact on adjacent healthy teeth
  • Improved speech and eating comfort
  • High success rate of over 95%

Frequently Asked Questions

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so you will not feel pain during surgery. Post-operative discomfort is minimal and can be managed with prescribed medication. Most patients report less discomfort than expected.

With proper oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups, dental implants can last a lifetime. The crown may need replacement after 10-15 years due to normal wear, but the implant itself is designed to be permanent.

Most adults with good general health are candidates for dental implants. Adequate jawbone density is needed, though bone grafting can help. A thorough consultation will determine the best approach for your specific situation.
